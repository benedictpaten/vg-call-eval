# Re-sweeping the caller's parameters after the mixture change

`--mismap-max` and `--mismap-min` were tuned against a model whose mixture weights were
flat. That model has been replaced, and `--mismap-min` was *directly* implicated in the
failure the new mixture fixes — lowering it recovered a third of the lost heterozygous
deletions on its own — so its old optimum was partly compensating for a defect that no
longer exists. Carrying the settings across unexamined is the mistake the original cap
sweep refused to make when it declined to carry "floor 0.05 is optimal" through a cap
change.

Searched on **chr20**, both graphs; validated on **chr6**, both graphs. Generated by
`param_sweep.py`, scored by `score_vcf.py` on both benchmarks at once.

**Conclusion: nothing moves.** The cap is inert above 0.5, the floor's optimum has not
shifted, and `--read-weight` turns out to be incapable of changing a genotype at all.

---

## `--mismap-max` is now inert above 0.5

The cap was the big win of the previous round — moving it 0.1 → 0.5 was worth more than
anything else on the 34-haplotype graph. It is now flat.

chr20-34hap, at fixed floor 0.02:

| cap | 0.5 | 0.7 | 0.9 | 0.99 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SV F1 | 0.4574 | 0.4588 | 0.4549 | 0.4561 |
| small-variant GT F1 | 0.9547 | 0.9546 | 0.9543 | 0.9543 |

chr20-4hap, same slice: SV F1 0.4907 / 0.4890 / 0.4903 / 0.4903, small-variant GT F1
0.9489 / 0.9488 / 0.9487 / 0.9486. Spread of 0.0044 and 0.0003 respectively, with no
consistent ordering between the two graphs — noise.

Why it went inert. The clamp only binds on reads whose phred-derived `e_r` exceeds it,
and at 0.5 that is already just MAPQ 0, 1 and 2 (`phred_to_prob(1) = 0.79`). Raising it
further affects a shrinking set of reads that carry almost no information either way.
0.99 is the practical ceiling: vg enforces the open interval `(0,1)`, and at exactly 1
every read's term collapses to `ln(1) = 0` for every genotype, so reads would contribute
nothing at all.

**No change.** 0.5 is not special, but nothing above it is better either, and a default
should not move on noise.

## `--mismap-min` trades the two benchmarks, and 0.02 is still the right compromise

The floor is the live axis. It caps what a junction-spanning read can contribute at
`ln(0.5 / floor)`, which is precisely the evidence large heterozygous deletions depend
on — so raising it mutes them, and lowering it lets low-quality reads speak.

| chr20-34hap | **0.02** | **0.4574** | 0.9547 | 0.9766 | 0.303 |
| chr20-34hap | 0.05 | 0.4508 | **0.9573** | 0.9750 | 0.152 |
| chr20-34hap | 0.10 | 0.4442 | 0.9537 | 0.9694 | 0.121 |

| chr20-4hap | **0.02** | 0.4907 | 0.9489 | 0.9757 | 0.212 |

### The case for 0.01, and why it was not taken

Moving 0.02 → 0.01 is consistent across all four datasets: heterozygous deletion recall
above 1 kb gains **+0.03 to +0.08**, small-variant genotype F1 loses **0.001 to 0.003**,
and aggregate SV F1 barely moves (−0.0008 to +0.0027). Measured only on the class the
model is worst at, that looks like a good trade.

It was not taken, for two reasons. Aggregate SV F1 does not improve — the gain is
confined to a sub-class, and is paid for out of the benchmark the caller is otherwise
strongest on. More importantly, buying deletion recall by loosening the floor is
*exactly* the pattern that was just removed: using a mismapping knob to compensate for a
model that cannot see missing coverage. The right fix for the remaining deletion deficit
is a depth term, not a detuned floor, and lowering the floor now would make that fix
harder to measure by partially masking what it is meant to correct.

## `--read-weight` cannot change a genotype

This one is not a tuning result but a fact about the model, and it invalidates a planned
piece of work.

| `--read-weight` | 0.5 | 0.75 | 1.0 | 1.5 | 2.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SV F1, small-variant F1, every SV class, het fraction | identical | identical | identical | identical | identical |
| median GQ (chr20-4hap) | 47 | — | 95 | — | 190 |

Byte-identical genotypes across the range, on both graphs, while GQ scales exactly with
`w` and GL exactly with `w` in log space (−0.30103 at 0.5 against −1.20412 at 2.0 — a
factor of four for a factor of four in `w`). The flag is wired correctly; the parameter
simply cannot do what fitting it assumed.

The reason is one line of algebra. `read_weight` multiplies every genotype's
log-likelihood by the same constant, and `argmax_G w·LL(G) = argmax_G LL(G)` for any
`w > 0`. It rescales confidence and nothing else.

Two consequences:

- **Stage 4b as specified cannot work.** Fitting `read_weight` against accuracy is
  measuring a quantity that provably cannot move, because every metric here is scored
  across all GQ thresholds. It has to be fitted against a *calibration* objective — does
  a GQ of `q` correspond to an error rate of `10^(-q/10)`? — which is a different
  experiment with a different truth requirement.
- **The design's description of it was wrong.** It was documented as an
  effective-sample-size correction addressing "a minority of correlated mismapped reads
  outvoting a correct homozygous call". It cannot stop anything outvoting anything.
  Correlated reads are a real problem and the correction is still the right idea, but to
  act on genotypes it has to enter the *per-read* term, as `e_r` does and as the mixture
  weights now do. Corrected in `allele_likelihood.hpp`.

Note also that GQ saturates at 256 and at `w = 2.0` the 90th percentile is already
there, so raising `w` destroys GQ's dynamic range before it improves any calibration.

## What this cost, and what it bought

24 grid points plus 8 high-floor points plus 10 read-weight points on chr20, and 6
validation points on chr6 — about four hours. It changed no default. That is the
expected outcome of a re-sweep more often than not, and the alternative was carrying
three settings across a model change on the assumption that they still transferred,
having just spent a week proving that one of them was compensating for a bug.

# Re-sweeping the caller's parameters after the mixture change

`--mismap-max` and `--mismap-min` were tuned against a model whose mixture weights were
flat. That model has been replaced, and `--mismap-min` was *directly* implicated in the
failure the new mixture fixes — lowering it recovered a third of the lost heterozygous
deletions on its own — so its old optimum was partly compensating for a defect that no
longer exists. Carrying the settings across unexamined is the mistake the original cap
sweep refused to make when it declined to carry "floor 0.05 is optimal" through a cap
change.

Searched on **chr20**, both graphs; validated on **chr6**, both graphs. Generated by
`param_sweep.py`, scored by `score_vcf.py` on both benchmarks at once.

**Two of three moved.** `--mismap-max` goes 0.5 → 0.7, `--read-weight` is removed
outright, and `--mismap-min` stays at 0.02.

---

## `--mismap-max`: 0.5 → 0.7

The cap was the big win of the previous round — moving it 0.1 → 0.5 was worth more than
anything else on the 34-haplotype graph. The remaining gain is far smaller, but real.

chr20-34hap, at fixed floor 0.02:

| cap | 0.5 | 0.7 | 0.9 | 0.99 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SV F1 | 0.4574 | 0.4588 | 0.4549 | 0.4561 |
| small-variant GT F1 | 0.9547 | 0.9546 | 0.9543 | 0.9543 |

chr20-4hap, same slice: SV F1 0.4907 / 0.4890 / 0.4903 / 0.4903, small-variant GT F1
0.9489 / 0.9488 / 0.9487 / 0.9486. Spreads of 0.0044 and 0.0003, and on this slice alone
the two graphs do not even agree on an ordering.

0.99 is the practical ceiling: vg enforces the open interval `(0,1)`, and at exactly 1
every read's term collapses to `ln(1) = 0` for every genotype, so reads would contribute
nothing at all.

Read one slice at a time it looks like noise, and on first reading it was called noise.
Read the whole surface and it is not:
**0.7 beats 0.5 on structural-variant F1 in 8 of 10 chr20 slices**, by more as the floor
rises (+0.0014 at floor 0.02, +0.0094 at floor 0.20). At the shipped floor of 0.02,
across all four datasets:

| dataset | SV F1 @0.5 | @0.7 | Δ | small-var GT Δ | het DEL 1k+ Δ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| chr20-4hap | 0.4907 | 0.4890 | −0.0017 | −0.0001 | +0.000 |
| chr20-34hap | 0.4574 | 0.4588 | +0.0014 | −0.0001 | +0.000 |
| chr6-4hap | 0.5532 | 0.5542 | +0.0010 | −0.0001 | +0.000 |
| chr6-34hap | 0.4896 | **0.4963** | **+0.0067** | −0.0000 | **+0.016** |

Mean +0.0018, three of four datasets up, small variants unmoved to four decimal places.

**Changed to 0.7**, on the strength of a mechanism as much as the numbers.
`phred_to_prob` gives 0.501 at MAPQ 3 and 0.631 at MAPQ 2, so a cap of 0.5 clamps every
read at or below MAPQ 3 — it tells a read the mapper thinks is probably misplaced that it
is no worse than a coin flip. 0.7 lets MAPQ 2 and 3 express themselves, and 0.9 and 0.99
gain nothing further because almost nothing is left to unclamp.

The effect is small and chr20-4hap disagrees. It is defensible because it is free: the
small-variant cost is at or below 0.0001 everywhere.

**One in-tree test moved, and it was informative.** `18_vg_call.t` asserts a star-allele
`GQ > 100` at a nested site. The genotype stays correct (`1/0`) at every cap; only GQ
moves, and steeply — **161, 83, 24** at caps 0.5, 0.7, 0.9. Every read in that fixture
comes from `vg sim` and carries **MAPQ 0**, so `e_r` equals the cap for all 230 of them
and the assertion was measuring the cap rather than the ploidy handling it is named for.
That invocation now pins `--mismap-max 0.5`. The general point is worth keeping: the
in-tree read-likelihood tests all run on simulated MAPQ-0 reads, so they exercise the
model in a regime — every read maximally unreliable — that never occurs on real data.

## `--mismap-min` trades the two benchmarks, and 0.02 is still the right compromise

The floor is the live axis. It caps what a junction-spanning read can contribute at
`ln(0.5 / floor)`, which is precisely the evidence large heterozygous deletions depend
on — so raising it mutes them, and lowering it lets low-quality reads speak.

| dataset | floor | SV F1 | small-variant GT F1 | SNV | het DEL 1k+ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| chr20-34hap | 0.01 | 0.4566 | 0.9521 | 0.9769 | 0.333 |
| chr20-34hap | **0.02** | **0.4574** | 0.9547 | 0.9766 | 0.303 |
| chr20-34hap | 0.05 | 0.4508 | **0.9573** | 0.9750 | 0.152 |
| chr20-34hap | 0.10 | 0.4442 | 0.9537 | 0.9694 | 0.121 |
| chr20-34hap | 0.20 | 0.4294 | 0.9332 | 0.9485 | 0.091 |
| chr20-4hap | 0.01 | **0.4920** | 0.9479 | 0.9762 | 0.242 |
| chr20-4hap | **0.02** | 0.4907 | 0.9489 | 0.9757 | 0.212 |
| chr20-4hap | 0.05 | 0.4886 | **0.9492** | 0.9739 | 0.121 |
| chr20-4hap | 0.10 | 0.4847 | 0.9448 | 0.9684 | 0.091 |
| chr20-4hap | 0.20 | 0.4687 | 0.9247 | 0.9480 | 0.061 |

Small-variant F1 has an interior optimum at 0.02–0.05 and falls off a cliff at 0.20 —
which is worth recording, because 0.20 was originally rejected only because the cap was
too low to express it, leaving open the possibility that it was unfairly dismissed. It
was not: at cap 0.5 it costs 0.02 small-variant GT F1.

**chr6 validates it**, at cap 0.5:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| chr6-4hap | 0.01 | **0.5559** | 0.9575 | **0.9822** | **0.525** |
| chr6-4hap | **0.02** | 0.5532 | **0.9586** | 0.9819 | 0.443 |
| chr6-4hap | 0.05 | 0.5479 | 0.9584 | 0.9801 | 0.230 |
| chr6-34hap | 0.01 | 0.4896 | 0.9593 | 0.9796 | **0.492** |
| chr6-34hap | **0.02** | 0.4896 | 0.9615 | 0.9794 | 0.443 |
| chr6-34hap | 0.05 | 0.4875 | **0.9627** | 0.9775 | 0.213 |

### The case for 0.01, and why it was not taken

Moving 0.02 → 0.01 is consistent across all four datasets: heterozygous deletion recall
above 1 kb gains **+0.03 to +0.08**, small-variant genotype F1 loses **0.001 to 0.003**,
and aggregate SV F1 barely moves (−0.0008 to +0.0027). Measured only on the class the
model is worst at, that looks like a good trade.

It was not taken, for two reasons. Aggregate SV F1 does not improve — the gain is
confined to a sub-class, and is paid for out of the benchmark the caller is otherwise
strongest on. More importantly, buying deletion recall by loosening the floor is
*exactly* the pattern that was just removed: using a mismapping knob to compensate for a
model that cannot see missing coverage. The right fix for the remaining deletion deficit
is a depth term, not a detuned floor, and lowering the floor now would make that fix
harder to measure by partially masking what it is meant to correct.

## `--read-weight` cannot change a genotype — removed

This one is not a tuning result but a fact about the model. It invalidated a planned
piece of work and the parameter has been deleted.

| `--read-weight` | 0.5 | 0.75 | 1.0 | 1.5 | 2.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SV F1, small-variant F1, every SV class, het fraction | identical | identical | identical | identical | identical |
| median GQ (chr20-4hap) | 47 | — | 95 | — | 190 |

Byte-identical genotypes across the range, on both graphs, while GQ scales exactly with
`w` and GL exactly with `w` in log space (−0.30103 at 0.5 against −1.20412 at 2.0 — a
factor of four for a factor of four in `w`). The flag is wired correctly; the parameter
simply cannot do what fitting it assumed.

The reason is one line of algebra. `read_weight` multiplies every genotype's
log-likelihood by the same constant, and `argmax_G w·LL(G) = argmax_G LL(G)` for any
`w > 0`. It rescales confidence and nothing else.

Two consequences:

- **Stage 4b as specified cannot work.** Fitting `read_weight` against accuracy is
  measuring a quantity that provably cannot move, because every metric here is scored
  across all GQ thresholds. It has to be fitted against a *calibration* objective — does
  a GQ of `q` correspond to an error rate of `10^(-q/10)`? — which is a different
  experiment with a different truth requirement.
- **The design's description of it was wrong.** It was documented as an
  effective-sample-size correction addressing "a minority of correlated mismapped reads
  outvoting a correct homozygous call". It cannot stop anything outvoting anything.
  Correlated reads are a real problem and the correction is still the right idea, but to
  act on genotypes it has to enter the *per-read* term, as `e_r` does and as the mixture
  weights now do. Corrected in `allele_likelihood.hpp`.

Note also that GQ saturates at 256 and at `w = 2.0` the 90th percentile is already
there, so raising `w` destroys GQ's dynamic range before it improves any calibration.

**Removed from vg.** It had no test coverage, and removal is verified byte-identical to
the previous default across 104,495 chr20 records. The idea behind it is still right —
correlated reads do overstate the evidence — but to act on genotypes a correction has to
enter the *per-read* term, as `e_r` does and as the mixture weights do. A per-read `w_r`
that down-weights mates and pile-up reads would do it; a global scalar provably cannot.
Anyone wanting to rescale confidence should get a knob that says so.

# Re-searching again, after λ was corrected

The depth term's weight had never been searched against the λ the caller now uses.
`depth_grid.py`, 27 points: a 15-point `w_d` × floor grid on chr20-4hap, six candidate points
on chr20-34hap, six validation points on chr6 both graphs.

**Why these two axes together.** `e_r` now enters the objective in three places — the per-read
background term, the observation `N_eff = Σ(1 − e_r)`, and the local rate `c(s)`, which carries
the same weighting so the correction stays relative. `--mismap-min` therefore moves the depth
term as well as the read term, by more than one route, and coordinate descent on a surface like
that finds whichever corner it started nearest.

Deliberately excluded: `--depth-quality`, which cannot change a genotype and so is free to sweep
offline afterwards; `--depth-ploidy`, which is structural; `--depth-count-raw`, already settled on
all four datasets; and the rate's window width, which gets its own scan below.

## `--depth-term`: 0.25 survives, but 0.1 is the better default

Structural-variant F1 at floor 0.02, cap 0.7:

| `w_d` | chr20-4hap | chr20-34hap | chr6-4hap | chr6-34hap | mean |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.4890 | 0.4588 | 0.5542 | 0.4963 | 0.4996 |
| **0.1** | 0.4998 | **0.4655** | 0.5616 | **0.5059** | **0.5082** |
| **0.25** | **0.5011** | 0.4627 | **0.5645** | 0.5053 | **0.5084** |
| 0.5 | 0.4943 | — | — | — | — |
| 1.0 | 0.4894 | — | — | — | — |

`w_d` is unimodal and turns over sharply above 0.25: on chr20-4hap at floor 0.01, true positives go
370 → 385 across the range while false positives go 354 → 405.

The mean is a tie, and the structure underneath is not noise: **the 4-haplotype graphs prefer 0.25
and the 34-haplotype graphs prefer 0.1, two for two, on both chromosomes.** More alleles means more
genotypes for λ to discriminate between, and a heavier weight starts overfitting to depth — on
chr6-34hap the step to 0.25 buys 9 true positives for 26 false ones, where on chr6-4hap it buys 13
for 17.

**0.1 is the recommendation:** aggregate F1 gives up nothing, it wins on precision, it wins on the
richer graph, and the graph-dependence points toward smaller weights as haplotype counts rise — the
direction pangenomes are going. The cost is explicit. Heterozygous deletion recall above 1 kb:

| dataset | `w_d`=0 | 0.1 | 0.25 | `poisson-z` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| chr20-4hap | 0.212 | 0.394 | 0.394 | 0.364 |
| chr20-34hap | 0.303 | 0.424 | 0.424 | 0.333 |
| chr6-4hap | 0.443 | 0.689 | **0.754** | 0.836 |
| chr6-34hap | 0.459 | 0.656 | **0.738** | 0.787 |

0.25 returns 0.065–0.082 more of the class the term was built for. If that class is the objective
rather than aggregate SV F1, 0.25 is the right choice. Small-variant genotype F1 is identical to four
decimal places at every point on all four datasets.

## The floor's trade dissolved, exactly as predicted

The section above rejected `--mismap-min` 0.01 on the grounds that *"buying deletion recall by
loosening the floor is exactly the pattern that was just removed… the right fix for the remaining
deletion deficit is a depth term, not a detuned floor."* That was written before the term existed.

Heterozygous deletion recall above 1 kb, chr20-4hap:

| `w_d` | floor 0.01 | floor 0.02 | floor 0.05 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.273 | 0.212 | 0.121 |
| ≥0.1 | **0.394** | **0.394** | 0.364–0.394 |

At `w_d = 0` the familiar gradient is there. With the term on, the floor stops mattering — the term
has taken over the job the floor was doing. The same happens on chr20-34hap: 0.303 / 0.152 at
`w_d = 0`, and 0.424 at both floors with the term on.

**Floor stays at 0.02.** Floor 0.05 wins SV F1 at `w_d = 0.1` on chr20-4hap (0.5023) and
small-variant ALL genotype F1 (0.9489), but costs 0.0020 SNV genotype F1 — about 150 records on
75,000, on the largest class. It is closer than that makes it sound: on chr20-34hap the ALL-class
gain is larger (+0.0023) and the SNV cost smaller (−0.0018), so the two graphs disagree about the
sign of the net. 0.02 is a compromise here rather than a clear optimum.

## The harm check, and what it can and cannot show

λ grows with allele length, so an anomalously large read count mechanically favours whichever
genotype presents the most sequence. That is a preference for long alleles, not a rejection of the
site, and it is how this term does damage — Stage 0's kill criterion. It cleared at `w_d = 0.25`
then, but the anchor constant used to damp λ's length dependence and no longer does.

Records with a called allele ≥ 50 bp at pile-up sites (`DR > 3`), chr20-4hap:

| `w_d` | large calls at `DR > 3` | median footprint | mean \|ln DR\| over large calls |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 17 | 122 | 0.584 |
| 0.1 | 17 | 122 | 0.560 |
| 0.25 | 17 | 122 | 0.546 |
| 0.5 | 16 | 124 | 0.532 |
| 1.0 | 12 | 124 | 0.509 |

No long-allele preference: the footprint is flat and the *count* of large calls at pile-ups falls.
The first version of this check ran over all records and reported a median footprint of 2 bp at every
point — true, and useless, because pile-up sites are overwhelmingly SNVs, so it was measuring the
wrong population.

The falling `|ln DR|` column is **not** evidence the term is right: it is the quantity the term
optimises, so it would fall by construction. The F1 and class-recall tables are the evidence.

`medDR` held at 0.976–0.988 across all 27 points, so λ's centring is insensitive to both axes.

## The rate's neighbourhood is saturated

There was no knob for this. The neighbourhood came from the read source's own fetch window,
with a caller-side fallback only for sources that have none, so it could only be varied through
`--read-window`. chr20-4hap at
`w_d = 0.1`: SV F1 **0.4961 / 0.4998 / 0.5008** at 1024 / 4096 / 16384. A 1024-node window is about
30 kb, narrow enough that the rate's variance shows; by 4096 it has converged and widening buys one
record. Confounded, since `--read-window` also sets fetch and cache granularity — but the confound
runs against the conclusion, because the wider window costs more per fetch and scored no better.

## What this cost, and what it bought

24 grid points plus 8 high-floor points plus 10 read-weight points on chr20, and 8
validation points on chr6 — about five hours, plus a full re-run of every arm on the new
default. It moved one default by +0.0018 mean SV F1, deleted one parameter, and left one
alone.

That is a thin return read as tuning, and the wrong way to read it. The re-sweep found
that a shipped knob could not do what it was documented to do, that a second knob's
optimum sat one step away from where it had been left, and that a passing in-tree test
was asserting something other than its own name. None of those were going to surface
from carrying the settings forward on the assumption that they still transferred —
which is exactly what had just been proven wrong about one of them.
